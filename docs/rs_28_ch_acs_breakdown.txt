================================================================================
   RS_28 -- COLOURFUL HELL: THE COMPLETE ACS BREAKDOWN.  2026-08-05.
   Every script and every function in CH's ACS, what it does, who calls it,
   and what a ZScript rebuild would have to contain.
================================================================================

WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS

  Every previous import of this mod stripped ACS calls without recording
  what they did, and real mechanics were lost each time. rs_19 established
  THAT this happened and estimated the scale across CH *and* CHP. This file
  is the other half: the exhaustive, line-level pass over CH's ACS only, so
  that no future import has to guess.

  RELATIONSHIP TO rs_19_acs_inventory.txt
    rs_19 is the survey and the decision record (what is worth porting, in
    what order, and why the "empty DECORATE wrapper" reasoning was wrong).
    rs_28 is the reference. Where the two disagree on a detail, rs_28 was
    measured directly against the source on 2026-08-05 and the disagreement
    is called out in-line. rs_19's *conclusions* are not overturned by
    anything here.
    THIS FILE COVERS CH ONLY. CHP's ACS (CHACS2, CHSett2, WVileEX, Bosses,
    NICEICE, commonFuncs2, miscFuncs2) is NOT in scope and is NOT documented
    here. rs_19 remains the only record of those.

  SEARCH METHOD FOR THE CALLER MAP -- stated because CLAUDE.md records two
  traps that have produced false negatives on this codebase before:
    * Every search was CASE-INSENSITIVE. CH writes `Actor`/`ACTOR` and
      `CallACS`/`callacs` interchangeably.
    * No pattern was anchored to line start. Most of CH's decorate is inline
      one-line state entries, so `^\s*THING` matches almost nothing.
    * Call verbs searched: CallACS, ACS_NamedExecute, ACS_NamedExecuteAlways,
      ACS_NamedExecuteWithResult, ACS_NamedTerminate, ACS_NamedSuspend,
      ACS_Execute and every ACS_Execute* variant.
    * 338 call sites found by a raw `grep -o` count; 338 attributed to an
      enclosing actor by a separate brace-free actor-tracking pass. The two
      numbers agreeing is the only cross-check available and it is a weak
      one -- it proves the attribution pass saw every site, not that every
      attribution is right. Twelve attributions were then verified by hand
      against the file; all twelve were correct.
    * Zero numeric `ACS_Execute(<number>)` calls exist anywhere in CH.

    THE ONE OVERLAP: RawketTypeCH is counted in BOTH (a) and (b), and is

    neither category alone describes it. Every other script sits in exactly

  CLASSIFICATION OF THE 117 FUNCTIONS

    Reachable from any of CH's own 80 scripts        4 of 117
      -- ProjInt_Brute, and the three helpers it calls: abs, sq, FixedAngMod
    Not reachable from any CH script              113 of 117

    This was computed, not eyeballed: a call graph was built from the
    stripped-of-comments bodies of all 80 scripts and all 117 functions, and
    breadth-first reachability was run from the scripts. See section 5(d)
    for what "not reachable" does and does not mean -- these are `#library`
    exports and CH ships them whole; they are dead *within CH*.

4.1  SECOND-ORDER REACH -- THE MAP ABOVE UNDERSTATES SOME AND OVERSTATES OTHERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The caller map lists the actor whose state contains the call. Four cases
  where that is not the whole story, and one where it is misleading:

  INHERITANCE WIDENS THE REACH.
    DropBaseAmmo (CH_AmmoUps) is a base class. Eight subclasses inherit its
    `Scripted` state without redefining it: CH_ClipBox, CH_ShellBox,
    CH_Shell, CH_CellPack, CH_Cell, CH_RocketBox, CH_RocketAmmo, CH_Clip.
    So CH_AmmoUps effectively gates nine ammo drop classes, not one.
    DropBaseItem (CH_Extras) has twelve subclasses, but all twelve override
    `Scripted` themselves and so already appear in the map by name.
    ScootDropChecker (MakeScoutCry) is likewise a base class: it is never
    itself a DropItem anywhere. Its subclass `implyingclip` inherits the
    check and IS dropped -- by Barons, Cacodemons and Chaingunners among
    others. MakeScoutCry therefore runs on many clip drops, not once.

  BUFF TOKENS ARE HANDED OUT AT RANGE -- THE REAL CALLER IS THE THROWER.
    Every buff/debuff script is reached through a CustomInventory whose only
    body is the ACS call. The script's ACTIVATOR is the RECEIVING monster,
    not the thrower. The thrower is:

5(b)  REAL GAMEPLAY LOGIC -- 15 of 80.  THIS IS THE SECTION THAT MATTERS.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Fifteen scripts change what happens in the world:
    CybMissile  BaronMissile  RawketTypeCH  MakeScoutCry
    BrownMindCommand  BrownVileCommand  BrownImpCommand  BrownRevSPEED
    BrownDinner  BBaronSlow  PERAGE  PESPEED  PEHULK
    RevBuffEx  RevKillEx

  Plus one function, ProjInt_Brute, which carries more real logic than any
  of them and is described first.

  FatMissile and ImpMissile are DEAD (5(d)) and are NOT among the fifteen,
  but their parameters are recorded at the end of this section because if
  the port ever rebuilds CybMissile/BaronMissile it will want its siblings'
  numbers, and they exist nowhere else.

  A STRUCTURAL DEFECT SHARED BY EIGHT OF THEM -- READ THIS FIRST.
    BrownMindCommand, BrownVileCommand, BrownImpCommand, BrownRevSPEED,
    BBaronSlow, PERAGE, PESPEED, PEHULK and RevBuffEx all open with
    `int Nope;` and guard their body with `if (Nope == 0)`, setting
    `Nope = 1` partway through as if it were a re-entrancy lock. It is not.
    `Nope` is a SCRIPT-LOCAL, zero-initialised on every execution, so the
    guard always passes and the "lock" is never observed by any other copy
    of the script. Consequence: a monster that receives the token twice runs
    two overlapping copies; the second reads the ALREADY-BUFFED value as
    `normal`, and when the first expires it restores the pre-buff value
    while the second later restores the buffed one. The base stat drifts.
    A ZScript rebuild must use a real per-actor field or a Powerup's own
    stacking rules; do not transliterate the `Nope` pattern.

    BBaronSlow takes this further: its guard reads `if (Nope == 3)`, which
    with a zero-initialised local is never true. THE SCRIPT'S BODY NEVER
    EXECUTES. It is called (Barons.txt:270/273/276) and does nothing.

  A SECOND DEFECT -- FIXED-POINT CONFUSION IN THE SPEED BUFFS.
    APROP_SPEED and APROP_DamageMultiplier are FIXED-POINT properties: 1.0
    is 65536. Where a script writes a bare integer it is writing 1/65536 of
    what it appears to write.
      PESPEED, BrownImpCommand:  SetActorProperty(APROP_SPEED, normal+10)
        adds 10 raw units = 10/65536 ~= 0.00015 map units per tic.
        Imperceptible. THE REAL EFFECT OF THESE TWO SCRIPTS IS THE
        +ALWAYSFAST FLAG (which makes the actor use its FastSpeed) AND, for
        BrownImpCommand, THE THRUST. Not the arithmetic.
      BrownRevSPEED:  `normal+7` -- same, and this script sets NO flag and
        applies NO thrust, so as written it is effectively a 210-tic no-op.
      BBaronSlow:  `normal*0.5` is an integer multiply by 32768, not a
        FixedMul -- it would overflow rather than halve. Moot, since the
        body never runs.
      RevBuffEx:  sets APROP_SPEED to 18.0 (correct fixed-point) and
        DamageMultiplier to 1.5 (correct), but RESTORES DamageMultiplier to
        the integer `1`, i.e. 1/65536. As written, a revenant that receives
        this buff ends up doing effectively zero damage after the 300 tics
        elapse. (It restores speed correctly, from the saved value.)
      PERAGE and the DamageFactor scripts (0.50, 0.65, 0.25) use proper
        fixed-point literals and are correct.

    THIS IS AN INFERENCE FROM ZDoom's DOCUMENTED APROP SEMANTICS, not
    something CH states, and it was not verified in-engine. It is stated
    here with its reasoning so the owner can weigh it. rs_19 described
    PESPEED as "+10 speed"; that description is repeated in this project's
    ZScript comments and, on this reading, is wrong about the mechanism
    while right that the script matters.

  THE THIRTEEN, IN DESCENDING ORDER OF HOW MUCH THERE IS TO REBUILD:

  1. ProjInt_Brute  (miscFuncs.acs:6) -- the function under CybMissile,
     BaronMissile, FatMissile and ImpMissile. Not a script, but it is the
     single largest piece of real logic in CH's ACS and nothing else here
     comes close.
     WHAT IT DOES. Solves for the time-of-flight `t` at which a projectile
     of fixed speed `spd`, launched from the shooter, will meet a target
     that keeps its current velocity. It does this by BRUTE-FORCE SEARCH,
     not algebra:
       - Establishes shooter TID and target TID, minting UniqueTIDs and
         restoring the originals afterwards if it had to.
       - Reads shooter XYZ and angle; applies `zoff` to Z, then `xoff`
         laterally (along angle -/+ 90 degrees depending on sign) and
         `yoff` forward/back.
       - Reads target XYZ + 36.0 (an eye-height offset; a commented line
         records 26.5 as the value that matches ZDoom's own
         Thing_ProjectileIntercept) and target velocity. IF THE TARGET
         LACKS +NOGRAVITY, Z VELOCITY IS ZEROED -- gravity-bound targets are
         predicted as if they will not rise or fall.
       - Then six successive refinement passes over `t`: pass 0 starts at
         t=1.0 with step 10.0 and 40 samples; pass 1 uses step 1.0 and 10
         samples, and BAILS OUT ENTIRELY if the best t so far exceeds 400.0
         ("stop trying to get closer if it's out of range, it's just a
         waste"); passes 2-4 use steps 0.1, 0.01, 0.001 with 10 samples;
         pass 5 uses a step of ONE RAW UNIT with 66 samples. Each pass
         re-centres on the best t found and keeps the sample minimising
         |spd - required_speed|.
       - `input_t` overrides the solved t entirely -- this is how FatMissile
         makes its second shot use the first shot's solution so the pair
         stays coherent.
       - `rand` requests a randomised solution. NOTE A BUG: the branch reads
         `if(rand){ random(1, sml_t); }` -- the random value is DISCARDED,
         never assigned to `t`, so `t` keeps whatever it held. The
         randomised path therefore does not randomise.
       - Applies the after-offsets axoff/ayoff/azoff (note axoff SUBTRACTS
         where xoff ADDED), then `angoff` rotates the final velocity vector
         -- and applies angoff TWICE, once to p_ang and once inside the
         cos/sin, so the effective rotation is 2*angoff.
       - Spawns the projectile, sets its target pointer to the shooter (so
         it does not collide with it) and its tracer to the victim,
         positions it, sets its angle and its full XYZ velocity, then
         restores the borrowed TIDs and returns `t`.

  2. BrownImpCommand -- pack mobility buff, 180 tics. Non-boss only.
     +ALWAYSFAST, a horizontal ThrustThing at a random angle with force
     1-12, a ThrustThingZ with force 1-12 in a random vertical direction,

================================================================================
7.  PORTING NOTE PER GAMEPLAY SCRIPT
================================================================================

  One line each for all fifteen scripts in 5(b), plus ProjInt_Brute.
  Grouped where the same rebuild covers several.

  ProjInt_Brute
     Do NOT port the brute-force search. ZScript has the algebra: solve the
     quadratic |targetPos + targetVel*t - shooterPos| = spd*t for t
     (a*t^2 + b*t + c with a = |tv|^2 - spd^2, b = 2*dot(rel, tv),
     c = |rel|^2), take the smaller positive root, and aim at
     targetPos + targetVel*t. Six refinement passes and 146 samples become
     one sqrt. Preserve the QUIRKS deliberately or drop them deliberately,
     but decide: the +36 unit eye offset, the zeroing of target Z velocity
     for non-NOGRAVITY targets, and the 400-unit bail-out are all
     behaviour the player feels. The `rand` path is a no-op in CH (the
     random value is discarded) -- do not reproduce the bug, and if a
     spread is wanted, add it explicitly. This project already has
     RS_MonsterAim per rs_19; check it against this description before
     assuming it matches.

  CybMissile / BaronMissile / FatMissile / ImpMissile
     These are four thin wrappers around the above with different speeds,
     offsets and projectile classes. In ZScript they are four calls to one
     shared `A_LeadingMissile(class, speed, xoff, yoff, zoff, angoff)`
     helper on the monster base class, not four functions. Two of the four
     are dead in CH; port the two live ones and keep the dead pair's
     parameters in a comment.

  PESPEED / BrownImpCommand / BrownRevSPEED  (the speed family)
     A Powerup subclass with a duration and, in the ZScript port, an
     explicit speed multiplier -- NOT an additive raw number. Give the
     powerup a real `Speed` factor rather than trying to reproduce
     ALWAYSFAST; +ALWAYSFAST on a monster mid-fight is a blunt instrument
     and ZScript can set `Speed` directly with a saved original on the
     actor. Duration 600 / 180 / 210 tics respectively. Use the Powerup's
     own stacking behaviour instead of the broken `Nope` guard, and restore
     from a per-actor saved field so overlapping applications cannot drift
     the base stat. BrownImpCommand additionally needs the two thrusts:
     `Thrust(frandom(1,12), random(0,359))` and a Z velocity kick.

  PERAGE
     Powerup with a 600-tic duration that sets DamageMultiplier 1.5 and
     +NOPAIN, restoring both from saved values. The most faithful of the
     family -- port it as written. Remember that CH clears NOPAIN
     unconditionally at the end; save the original flag state instead.

  PEHULK
     Powerup, 600 tics, DamageFactor 0.25 plus three flag changes
     (+QUICKTORETALIATE, -NODROPOFF, +DONTTHRUST). In ZScript, snapshot all
     three bools on apply and restore the snapshot on expiry rather than
     CH's unconditional set/clear. This is the one buff whose flags do real
     work -- DONTTHRUST in particular changes how the monster reads in a
     firefight.

  BrownMindCommand / BrownVileCommand  (the shield family)
     Powerup, 300 tics, DamageFactor 0.50 / 0.65. Trivial in ZScript.
     Keep the four spawned children (BrownMindBone1 / BrownVileRock4) as
     part of the DELIVERING actor, not the powerup -- in CH they are
     spawned by the CustomInventory, not by the script, and moving them
     would change who owns them.

  BrownDinner
     No clean 1:1: ThrustThingZ's additive semantics and the temporary
     +FLOAT do not map to a single ZScript call. Build it as a short-lived
     Powerup on the victim that sets bNOGRAVITY (or bFLOAT) on apply,
     applies `vel.z += 3.75` once, and clears the flag after 30 tics. The
     tricky part is that it targets the PLAYER; RS will want to decide
     whether a monster is allowed to launch the player at all before
     porting this one. That is a design call, not a porting call.

  RevBuffEx
     Powerup, 300 tics, Speed 18.0 and DamageMultiplier 1.5. Straight to
     port EXCEPT: do not reproduce the integer-`1` restore. Restore the
     saved multiplier. Also note the trigger is the EX Revenant's PAIN
     state, which in ZScript means overriding the Pain state or hooking
     DamageMobj -- the latter is cleaner and fires reliably even when
     PainChance does not.

  RevKillEx
     One line in ZScript: `recipient.DamageMobj(null, null, 999999,
     'None')` or simply `A_Die`. The porting question is not HOW but WHO --
     see 5(b) item 9. Resolve the A_RadiusGive filter question in-engine
     before building it, because "kills my summons" and "kills everything
     within 9999 units" are very different features.

  BBaronSlow
     Nothing to port -- the body never executes. If the mechanic is wanted,
     it must be designed fresh: a Powerup applied to PROJECTILES (the
     A_RadiusGive uses RGF_MISSILES) that halves their Speed for 210 tics.
     ZScript can do that cleanly by iterating a BlockThingsIterator for
     missiles and scaling their `vel`, which is more direct than an
     inventory token on a projectile.

  RawketTypeCH
     Delete it. In ZScript the rocket class reads the CVar in
     PostBeginPlay and sets its own DamageType, or -- better -- RS defines
     two rocket classes and picks one at spawn. Running a script per rocket
     to set one property is the ACS-shaped solution to a problem ZScript
     does not have.

  MakeScoutCry
     `bool(PlayerPawn.FindClass("RLArsenalThingo"))` -- or in modern
     ZScript, `(class<Actor>)("RLArsenalThingo") != null`. No spawn, no
     removal, no caching needed, no map-scope latch. CH's version spawns and
     removes a real actor at the map origin to test for a class's existence;
     ZScript can just ask.

================================================================================
8.  WHAT I COULD NOT DETERMINE -- STATED RATHER THAN GUESSED
================================================================================

  1. THE A_RadiusGive FILTER QUESTION (RevKillEx). Whether
     `A_RadiusGive("PowerRevEx",9999,RGF_MONSTERS|RGF_NOSIGHT,1,
     "BlackRevex3")` applies the "BlackRevex3" filter without RGF_EXFILTER
     or RGF_INCLUSIVEFILTER set. This decides whether the Black Revenant
     EX's death kills its own summons or every monster on the map. It needs
     the engine, not the source. Same question applies to the PowerRevEx2 /
     RevBuffEx site at radius 2800.

  2. THE FIXED-POINT READING. Section 5(b) argues that `normal+10` on
     APROP_SPEED is a no-op and that RevBuffEx's trailing `1` nearly zeroes
     a revenant's damage. That follows from ZDoom documenting APROP_Speed
     and APROP_DamageMultiplier as fixed-point, but I did not observe it in
     game. If it is wrong, PESPEED and BrownRevSPEED are larger buffs than
     I have described and RevBuffEx has no bug. Everything else in this
     document is independent of that reading.

  3. WHAT `quad()` IS FOR. commonFuncs.acs:357 evaluates
     `a*y^2 + b*y + c + y`. The trailing `+ y` makes it not a quadratic
     evaluation and no caller exists to disambiguate. Recorded as written;
     not guessed at.

  4. WHY `round` AND `ceil` ARE COMMENTED OUT. `ceil`'s body is
     `ftoi(toround + (1.0-1))`, which is not a ceiling operation. That is
     suggestive but it is not proof, and CH leaves no comment.
