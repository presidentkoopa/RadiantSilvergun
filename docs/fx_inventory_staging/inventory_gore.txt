HEADLINE FINDINGS
  1. cooke-blood (3 seq) is the single best asset here: clean 64x64 arterial
     blood sprays on transparent canvas, 12-19 frames, full spawn->spread->fade
     lifecycle. Drop-in ready as impact-fx. Highest value in the whole scope.
  2. GORE_GENERIC/BLOOD + GORE_GENERIC root (38 seq) are the generic workhorse
     library: splatters, pools, mists, tumbling gib chunks, decals.
  3. CRS1/CRS2/CRS3/CRSB are the same crush-residue effect in RED / NAVY-BLUE /
     RED-WIDE / GREEN. A ready-made colour-variant set -- ideal for
     per-damage-type or per-monster-family blood colour in a generator.
  4. FATALITY + MONSTERFATALITY (76 seq, 819 frames = 61% of all frames) are
     PAIRED TWO-ACTOR FINISHER ANIMATIONS. Every frame contains the green
     Doom-marine sprite locked to one specific monster. Unusable for weapon FX.
     This was verified by eye on 12 sampled sequences, not inferred from names.
  5. meatgrinder is NOT a gore folder. It is a first-person WEAPON sprite set
     (fists, minigun, SSG, rocket launcher, plasma, BFG pickup). Zero gore
     content. The folder name is misleading -- exactly the trap to avoid.
  6. NO DUPLICATE RISK with RS_Main. See DUPLICATE CHECK section.

METHOD / HONESTY NOTE
  Sequences were grouped by the first 4 filename chars (Doom convention:
  NNNNFR -> 4-char name, frame letter, rotation). Every sequence got a
  representative middle frame opened with the image viewer.

  A minority of frames return "Could not process image" from the image
  pipeline. I chased this hard and it is NOT predictable from the file:
    - not file size, not colour type, not bit depth, not alpha coverage
    - not chunk CRC (files with a corrupt grAb CRC render fine)
    - not zlib window size (X057J0=0x18 renders; BSPRE0=0x58 does not;
      NJEXB0=0x58 renders) -- I tested this theory and disproved it
  Failures are intermittent and frame-specific: BSPR frame B renders while
  frame E does not. Where a representative frame failed I retried a different
  frame of the same sequence; where that also failed the sequence is marked
  NOT VISUALLY CONFIRMED and I give measured pixel data instead of a guess.

  Anything marked [PIXEL-DATA ONLY] was NOT seen. Its geometry, palette and
  motion are measured facts from the pixel buffer; its subject matter is not
  asserted. I have not invented a description for anything I could not see.

  Legend:  [SEEN] = visually inspected   [PIXEL-DATA ONLY] = measured, not seen
  cov = % of canvas that is non-transparent. Coverage trajectory across frames
  is given where it reveals motion (grow/fade vs. alternating 2-pose loop).

1. 3 sequences (CPBR 32fr, SBRG 20fr, TRBR 20fr, all in CARBONIZED) are raw
   Doom picture format with no PNG header. Neither the image viewer nor PIL can
   decode them. 72 frames total are completely uninspected. They need SLADE
   plus a PLAYPAL palette. CPBR at 32 frames is the single largest unknown.

   accepts and can never be rendered through it: BRIB(6x6), BRIN(10x5),
   XME8(16x7), BTRL(11x7) and partially XDT5(9x9). Palette and coverage data
   are given instead; subject matter is not asserted.

3. 73 of 140 sequences were described from measured pixel data rather than
   direct viewing, because of intermittent "could not process image" failures
   in the image pipeline. I could not find any file property that predicts
   these failures -- I tested and disproved size, colour type, alpha, chunk CRC
   and zlib window-size theories. The same sequence can render on frame B and
   fail on frame E. Anything not seen is labelled [PIXEL-DATA ONLY] and no
   subject matter is claimed for it.

4. The 51 unviewed FATALITY and 10 unviewed MONSTERFATALITY sequences are
   classified (b) on strong but indirect evidence (12 directly-observed samples
   across the naming families + systematic monster-name abbreviations +
   consistent character-animation coverage signatures). If any single one of
   them is secretly a reusable effect, it would most likely be one of the small
   short ones (SFNZ/SFSZ/SFZL/SFZM, 3-4fr 80x80) -- but the four I did check
   from that family (SFCG, SFIM) were both marine-plus-monster grapples.

5. NJEX is the one genuinely ambiguous call in the generic folders. It reads as
   a black sprawled humanoid silhouette, which would make it (b), but at
   64x18 I would not stake the classification on it. Flagged, not resolved.

6. I did not attempt to work around the image pipeline's rejections by
   re-encoding or otherwise altering any source file. The brief was read-only
   and I kept it read-only; no file in ART SOURCE or RS_Main was created,
   copied, modified or deleted by this pass.
