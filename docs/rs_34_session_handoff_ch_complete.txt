THINGS THAT WERE SILENTLY BROKEN AND ARE NOT ANY MORE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every one of these passed every check in the repo while being wrong. That is
the pattern worth remembering, not the individual fixes.

  * TWO SOUNDS HAD NEVER PLAYED. bonsai/gunlevelup pointed at a path with no
    file extension; rs_allclear_ready pointed at a directory that does not
    exist. An unresolved sound in GZDoom is COMPLETELY INERT -- no error, no
    warning, no log line. The second one is the cadence "ready to fire" beep,
    i.e. the Plasma Rifle prototype for the arsenal-wide tell. Inaudible
    since the day it was written.
  * A SOUND FILE WAS SITTING IN THE SPRITES FOLDER. WASPSPLT.lmp, byte-
    identical to the real one in sounds/ch/. Its 8-char name parsed as
    sprite WASP frame S rotation P -- an invalid rotation -- corrupting the
    frame table of a live monster.
  * THE COMMON CYBERDEMON FIRED NOTHING. Its missile frame called an ACS
    lead-shot solver we do not have. Reading CH's own ACS SOURCE (which
    ships in CH\source\, next to the compiled .o the import worked from)
    showed the solver has a bug: `if(rand){ random(1, sml_t); }` discards
    the result, so lead time stays 0 and the shot collapses onto the
    target's current position. CH's cyberdemon does not lead. A plain aimed
    rocket is FAITHFUL, not a substitution.
  * ~30 SPRITE PREFIXES referenced art that does not exist -- in CH as well
    as here. Resolved with evidence: SGRN->HGRN (only *GRN* art in CH, and
    it is a grenade sprite), SPG2->SRG2 (a twin line in the demon file
    proves the spectre file is a copy that mistyped one character),
    VBAL->CBAL (all 152 CBAL references start at frame C, orphaning A and
    B -- exactly the residue one misspelling leaves), BRUR O-W (a 23-frame
    list copy-pasted onto a 14-frame sprite), plus a dozen single-character
    slips. SLGM F turned out never to have been missing at all: the art sat
    uncopied in ART SOURCE under a caret-escaped filename.

THE TWO DELIBERATE DEPARTURES FROM CH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both are the owner's rulings. DO NOT "correct" either back; both are
documented at their sites with the full derivation.

  1. THE HELL KNIGHT LEADS ITS SHOTS. CH's does not, but only because of the
     discarded-random bug above, which its baron and lost souls escape by
     passing a different argument. The asymmetry is a typo, not a design.
  2. SPRY -> BAL1 on the red demon's blood spray. The art exists in neither
     CH nor CHP; CH's three other blood bolts all draw BAL1, so this one now
     matches its own family instead of rendering nothing.

(A third, older ruling stands: GrayPE2's healed GrayDemon2 -> RS_GreyDemon2.)

NEXT SESSION STARTS HERE: BILLBOARD DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The in-world card works. It draws, it faces the player, and the compositor
paints a canvas texture (RSCARDA) every frame in RS_UIHandler.RenderOverlay
(zscript/systems/player/rs_ui.zs), which an engine billboard displays via
AttachBillboard on the spinning showcase stand at rs_ui.zs:652. Three faults
the owner reported from actually wearing the headset: it sits TOO HIGH and
needs to come down to HMD level; it renders BACKWARDS, i.e. it turns to face
you but its content is mirrored; and the card's design and the information it
presents are both first-draft and need workshopping rather than patching. The
height is two constants at the attach site (offset 56, size 44). The mirroring
is almost certainly a facing-normal or UV-winding sign error in the
orientation math rather than a canvas problem, because the text composites
correctly before it is handed to the billboard -- so look at the vector built
from the player's view, and at whether AttachBillboard expects a normal that
is being handed to it inverted.

The design conversation is the bigger half, and it should not start from the
current card. The real question is what an in-world panel is FOR in a game
played in VR, given a HUD already exists: a thing you lean toward and read is
a different instrument from a thing pinned to your face, and it earns its
place only by carrying what the HUD cannot -- comparison, history, or a
choice to make. The engine surface is already broad (payloads for panel,
texture, digits, glyph, ring and bar; a colour palette; AimBillboard returning
a hit and a UV, which is what makes a panel clickable), so the constraint is
taste rather than capability. Worth deciding early: how many panels may exist
at once, whether they are summoned or ambient, whether they follow the player
or stay where they are put, and what happens to one you walk away from. The
owner's next objectives after the card, in his order, are: make panels
clickable via AimBillboard uv -> row -> netevent; a drop triptych comparing a
dropped weapon against both held weapons with per-row diffs; a wheel skin with
fixed slots, ammo digits and magazine rings; and affix controls in-world.
